A concept born from Hockeytown pride

This is a concept design project. While I was in Ann Arbor, Michigan for my undergraduate studies, I became a huge fan of Detroit sports — so when I heard the PWHL was bringing a women's hockey team to the city, I was thrilled.

I started sketching ideas and researching Detroit's history, especially its deep automotive roots. That connection felt like the right thread to pull — the Red Wings' own branding has long leaned on the city's manufacturing legacy, and I wanted this new team's identity to carry that same lineage forward.

Research

Before any mark is made, the city has to be understood. Detroit carries contradictions: industrial decay and creative resilience, blue-collar roots and boundary-pushing culture. Its identity is not one thing — and neither should a hockey team's brand be.

Research covered two parallel tracks: the history of Detroit as a city and the history of hockey in Detroit. Both informed every decision that followed.

Detroit is a city defined by reinvention. From the auto industry that shaped American manufacturing, to Motown that gave the world a new sound, to a contemporary art and design scene that continues to emerge from its own ruins — Detroit refuses to be only what it was.

That tension — grit and grace, legacy and ambition — became the emotional core of this brand.

🏙️
History of Detroit
Founded in 1701 on the Detroit River, the city became the center of American automotive manufacturing in the early 20th century. The Big Three — Ford, GM, Chrysler — built not just cars but a working-class culture of pride and craft. Motown Records (est. 1959) transformed it into a cultural capital. Post-industrial challenges reshaped the city, yet a renaissance in arts, food, and design has redefined Detroit for a new generation.

History of Detroit Hockey
The Detroit Red Wings (est. 1926) are one of the Original Six NHL franchises and holders of 11 Stanley Cup championships. Detroit earned the nickname "Hockeytown" — not a marketing slogan, but a lived identity. The octopus toss at Joe Louis Arena, the retired jersey banners, the Gordie Howe legacy: hockey is civic religion here. The PWHL expansion represents a new chapter in that legacy, finally giving women's hockey a permanent home in one of the sport's most passionate cities.
